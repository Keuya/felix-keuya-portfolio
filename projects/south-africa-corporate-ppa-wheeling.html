South African corporate PPA and wheeling commercial case

A corporate buyer considering private renewable power needed a clear view of the contractual chain, delivered-energy economics and risks that sit between the headline PPA tariff and the customer meter.

01

Situation

South African wheeling transactions require more than a bilateral PPA. Grid connection, use-of-system arrangements, buyer account amendments, metering, settlement, network charges and municipal participation can all affect the commercial result.

02

Mandate

Build a representative corporate PPA and wheeling case that a developer, adviser, investor or corporate energy buyer could use to understand transaction structure, economics and diligence requirements.

03

Analysis completed

  • Mapped the generator, buyer, trader, Eskom and municipal roles across the contractual chain.
  • Separated the energy price from connection charges, use-of-system tariffs, administration costs, losses, balancing and residual supply.
  • Modelled delivered-energy economics against the buyer’s avoided grid tariff and load profile.
  • Reviewed volume, shape, take-or-pay, deemed energy, change-in-law and termination-payment provisions.
  • Tested curtailment, grid unavailability, settlement mismatch and municipal-consent scenarios.
  • Defined the credit package, security deposits, payment support and data requirements needed before commitment.

04

Decision value

The analysis shows whether a lower PPA energy price survives network charges, losses, profile mismatch and contractual risk, and identifies which interfaces need confirmation before the parties rely on projected savings.

Public case-study boundary

This is an independent representative case built from public market structures and commercial analysis. It is not presented as a completed client mandate. All project sizes, prices and contract terms are illustrative unless explicitly stated otherwise.
