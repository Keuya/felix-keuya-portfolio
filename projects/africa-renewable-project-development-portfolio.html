How project maturity was handled

An announced project, a modelled project and a financeable project were treated as different states

Across the work, project claims arrived with uneven evidence. A capacity could be supported by a technical design while the offtake remained a term sheet; a tariff could exist in a model while deductions and allocation were unresolved; a COD target could appear in a schedule before land, grid and financing conditions were complete.

Felix used explicit evidence states rather than filling gaps with confident prose:

  • Reported fact: supported by a current primary or authoritative source.
  • Draft or under negotiation: relevant to analysis but not treated as executed.
  • Client or sponsor assumption: used transparently and sensitivity-tested.
  • Analyst estimate: derived for screening and clearly labelled.
  • Research pending / not publicly disclosed: no positive conclusion until supplied.
Analyst note

The quality of portfolio analysis is not the number of projects in the table. It is the ability to stop a draft PPA, an estimated CAPEX and a sponsor target date being presented as one equally certain investment case.

Commercial questions by operating case

SPV buys diesel
Fuel cost enters project OPEX and diesel energy may be included in contracted revenue.
Offtaker bears diesel
Fuel is excluded from SPV cost and diesel energy should not inflate project revenue.
Offtaker supplies diesel
Supply, quality and availability risk sit outside the SPV but still affect service performance.
Higher BESS
More CAPEX and reduced diesel dependence; value depends on dispatch and lifecycle evidence.
Lower BESS
Lower initial cost but greater fuel and reliability exposure.
