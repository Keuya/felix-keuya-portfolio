01

The decision the pack had to support

The assignment was not a general market report. It had to help an investment principal decide whether African renewable power could fit an existing energy investment playbook, where the most credible entry points sat, and what should be tested before allocating further origination time.

The pack therefore connected investor fit, power demand, contract structure, development stage, equity-ticket size and bankability. It deliberately moved from a broad regional thesis to a short list of opportunity archetypes and then to a repeatable diligence framework.

02

Investment thesis framing

The first section explained why mine-tethered and industrial power could offer a more investable route than an undifferentiated utility-scale pipeline. The thesis centred on three linked questions:

  • Is there identifiable power demand tied to operating assets, expansions or processing requirements?
  • Can a private PPA, dedicated line or islanded system reduce dependence on weak utility credit and constrained transmission?
  • Can the project support institutional debt while preserving an acceptable equity return under yield, cost and delay stress?

The analysis translated familiar oil and gas concepts into renewable-energy equivalents: resource and land rights, PPA offtake, dedicated transmission, P50/P90 yield assessment, political-risk cover and long-tenor contracted revenue.

03

Four opportunity archetypes

The pack presented four market archetypes rather than claiming that named projects were immediately investable. Each was designed to show a demand signal, an appropriate power structure and the commercial reason further origination could be justified.

01

DRC Copperbelt

Mine-tethered solar plus BESS, typically 20–80 MW, with a direct-wire structure and corporate-credit focus rather than reliance on sovereign utility risk.

02

Zimbabwe lithium processing

Dedicated hybrid power for processing loads, with particular attention to hard-currency revenues, customer credit and the energy intensity of local beneficiation.

03

Guinea mining corridors

Anchor-load power infrastructure capable of scaling with refinery and corridor development, requiring careful staging, transmission and demand verification.

04

Tanzania graphite coast

Solar and storage or hybrid island-grid structures for remote processing clusters, with multi-offtaker risk diversification considered where practical.

04

The six-lens bankability screen

Every opportunity was required to pass the same six checks before it could move from an interesting market story to an investable diligence candidate.

02 · GridConnection and curtailment riskDirect wire or grid connection, dispatch priority, metering, losses and contractual treatment.
03 · RevenueTariff, escalation and FX exposurePPA currency, indexation, convertibility, hedging and stressed exchange-rate treatment.

05

What the client received

  • A three-page executive opportunity screen designed for a principal-level discussion.
  • A quantified investment box covering project size, ticket size, PPA tenor, leverage, entry stage and target offtaker type.
  • A concise market thesis linking industrial demand, transmission gaps and private-power structures.
  • Four prioritised opportunity archetypes with the demand rationale, commercial structure and major risk.
  • A six-lens bankability checklist capable of being reused for future opportunities.
  • A recommended alignment call covering geography, risk appetite, return threshold and deal-flow cadence.

06

How the pack would progress into transaction work

The opportunity screen was an origination filter, not a substitute for full diligence. A mandate progressing beyond this stage would require a project-specific financial model, verified load and tariff data, counterparty credit review, regulatory and grid diligence, technical yield and storage work, legal review and an evidence-backed investment memorandum.

This distinction matters: the value of an early screen is not that it proves an investment case. It prevents a team from spending transaction-level time on opportunities that do not meet its investment box.

Public case-study boundary

The original pack was confidential. This public summary removes the recipient, correspondence and client-specific strategy. Return ranges in an early-stage screen were indicative filters, not realised returns, valuations or promises. Named markets and sectors were opportunity hypotheses requiring independent verification before investment.
