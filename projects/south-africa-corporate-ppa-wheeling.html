Independent representative analysis

South African corporate PPA and wheeling commercial case

The case tests whether a corporate renewable-energy transaction still creates value after network losses, use-of-system charges, balancing, profile mismatch, residual grid supply and contractual risk are added to the headline PPA tariff.

Case classificationRepresentative case, not a completed client mandate

01

The commercial question

Does the proposed private-power structure reduce the buyer’s total electricity cost while providing the generator with a financeable revenue case, after all network, settlement and contractual interfaces are included?

02

Transaction structure mapped

Party or interfaceCommercial roleKey evidence required
Generator / project companyProduces energy and earns the contracted PPA priceEnergy study, connection, project cost, operating assumptions and credit case
Corporate buyerPurchases matched renewable energy and residual grid supplyInterval load, tariff bills, credit profile, sites and account structure
Network service providerTransports energy and applies losses and use-of-system chargesConnection, wheeling, tariff, metering and loss-factor arrangements
MunicipalityMay retain the buyer account and approve or administer wheelingMunicipal rules, tariffs, account amendments and settlement process
Trader or balancing partySupports aggregation, balancing, billing or settlementFees, imbalance treatment, data rights and credit support
Residual supplierSupplies energy not matched by the renewable plantGrid tariff, demand charges and residual-supply terms

Commercial conclusion: the headline PPA tariff is not the buyer’s delivered cost. The case remains attractive only after PPA energy, network charges, balancing and residual supply are reconciled against the avoided grid bill.
