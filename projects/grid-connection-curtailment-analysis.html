Anonymised live project workstream · Grid and wheeling · Southern Africa

The energy study said the plant could generate. The financing case still had to prove that the power could leave the site, reach the buyer and remain payable when the network failed.

Felix reviewed the connection and wheeling workstream for a utility-scale solar IPP alongside its financial model and project agreements. The work did not assume “grid connected” meant “fully bankable”: export capacity, interface works, losses, curtailment, outage treatment, metering and settlement each had to be traced into cash flow.

The failure mode

A plant can be technically complete and still miss its commercial operation date

The connection route involved more than a line on a layout. It required an agreed point of injection, bay and protection scope, meter hierarchy, ownership of network works, grid-code testing, wheeling and system-operation agreements, and a clear route from measured generation to the customer invoice.

The review separated three risks often blended into one assumption: connection delay before COD, technical losses during delivery and curtailment or outage after operations begin.

Analyst note

The model originally cared about annual MWh. Lenders care about paid MWh, the timing of the cash and the contract remedy when the missing energy was caused by someone other than the project company.

Documents and evidence that carried the answer

  • Connection and energy studies, single-line diagrams and proposed point of injection.
  • Network works scope, responsibility matrix, cost estimate and construction timetable.
  • Grid, wheeling, system-operation and operational-agent terms.
  • PPA delivery point, metering, curtailment, deemed-energy and payment provisions.
  • EPC interface, grid-code testing, energisation and delay-liquidated-damages schedule.
  • Financial model lines for losses, delay, curtailment, collection and reserves.

Energy-to-cash bridge

Four different quantities

Gross generation
Technical energy before plant availability, degradation and operating losses.
Available energy
Energy the plant can produce after asset-level operating adjustments.
Delivered energy
Energy that passes the export limit, network losses and curtailment constraints.
Paid energy
Metered or deemed energy accepted through the settlement and collection process.

Downside cases carried into finance

Not one generic curtailment haircut

Connection delay
Lost revenue, added development cost, financing fees and IDC.
Permanent export cap
Reduced P90 energy and lower debt capacity across the full tenor.
Technical losses
Separate deduction between injection and customer delivery.
Planned outage
Annual allowance with or without compensation.
Forced outage
Short-term revenue loss, collection delay and reserve use.
Economic curtailment
Volume stress with contract attribution and deemed-energy alternative.

The human judgement

Not every grid risk should be priced into a single lower yield

Blending grid losses, curtailment and availability into one energy haircut hides who controls each risk and which agreement should provide the remedy. Felix kept the items separate so the project team could distinguish a technical performance risk from a network event and a commercial collection problem.

That also improved lender questions. A project may tolerate ordinary technical losses, but not unlimited uncompensated curtailment or a connection scope that can move without a fixed cost and long-stop.

Decision reached

The model could only be relied on after the connection basis, network-work responsibility, loss factors, curtailment allocation, metering protocol and grid conditions precedent were aligned across the technical and commercial documents.
